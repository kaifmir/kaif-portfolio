Designing Houzy

Designed end to end, from first conversation to the moment a buyer decides

Houzy is India's first conversational AI for property search. I designed it end to end. First I built the foundations: its personality, conversation maps and chat interface. Then I took it from a text assistant to a place where buyers decide, with photos, follow-ups and the property page inside the conversation, across app, mobile web and desktop.

Problem, stakes, constraints3–8 MIN · 3 SLIDES MAX

Buyers think in sentences. Search made them think in filters.

The gap

  • Buyers describe homes in sentences ("something peaceful, close to my parents, good for a family"). Search needs exact filters and multiple attempts.
  • The existing WhatsApp bot couldn't show photos well, and its links broke when buyers moved to the app.

The stakes

  • The product team's own risk statement: Houzy could become "a novelty Q&A feature rather than a core property discovery tool".
  • Buyers increasingly expect a conversation, not a form, and competitors abroad had already launched one.

The constraints

  • An LLM decides the content: 1 photo or 16, an exact match or none, a reply in 2 seconds or 6.
  • Engineering bandwidth was tight and the ML output was still stabilising, so every pattern had to be cheap to build and safe to fail.
End to end

Two phases: trust the conversation, then decide inside it

Phase 1 · V1, Android app

Make a conversation buyers trust

  • Personality: a collaborative guide, not an authority. One question at a time, short messages, warm but professional.
  • Failure playbook: ambiguous, out-of-scope and frustrated queries each get a way forward.
  • Slot-based conversation maps, so Houzy knows when to answer, when to ask and when to recover.
  • The chat interface: first open, property cards, login only when acting, feedback, shortlist, loading and error states.
Phase 2 · V2, app + web

Make it the place buyers decide

  • The property page inside Houzy, with an ask box for that home.
  • Photos, floor plans, price trends and ratings as rich messages, not text dumps.
  • Follow-up pills, Seen/Contacted tags, dates and recovery when nothing matches.
  • From app-only to mobile web and desktop, as one chat system.

Spend about 60 seconds here. The personality and conversation design make the UI decisions make sense, but the panel is scoring craft, so get to the screens.

Four decisions, across both phases

For each one: what was broken, the options I weighed, what I chose and why.

01

A property card that earns trust before it shows the price

Phase 1. In chat, a card gets a fraction of the space of a search results card. I had to decide what a buyer needs to see first to believe an AI's recommendation, and how little it takes to act on it.

Option AA text list from the model. Cheap, but you can't verify it or act on it, and it reads like a guess.
Option BThe full search results card. Familiar, but two cards fill the screen and push the conversation away.
ChosenA compact card: RERA and status tags → BHK and project → area → locality → price → Learn more / Contact. The next card peeks in from the right.
  • RERA comes first because in this market, verification is the first question.
  • The price is the largest text, so it can be compared across cards at a glance.
  • Only Contact is filled purple: one primary action per card.
  • The peeking card tells you it swipes, with no dots or arrows.
  • Login appears as a sheet only when the buyer acts, and the thread is still there afterwards.
02

Bring the property page into the conversation

Phase 2. Tapping a card in Houzy redirected to the full property page. Two things broke. To compare the next home, buyers had to back all the way out and re-enter. And on the property page, they could no longer ask Houzy anything.

BeforeFull redirect. The explore loop dies, and the property page shows its own seller chat, a second chat that can't answer the same things.
OptionA lighter bottom-sheet quick view with key data. Cheaper, but it still sends the buyer out for anything deeper.
ChosenAn in-context overlay: swipe between the carousel's properties, and a floating ask box scoped to that home, answered by Houzy.
  • One conversational voice per screen. I removed the three seller-chat entry points, so it's always clear who you're talking to.
  • Close (×), not Back. The overlay feels temporary, so you know the chat is right underneath.
  • Starter questions by property type. New projects get possession, amenities, configurations and landmarks. Resale gets floor/facing and area. Rent gets price breakup, furnishing and parking.
  • Answer windows. Short answers return to the main chat. Long ones stay in a scrolling answer window over the page, capped in height so the hero image stays visible.
  • Trimmed page. Similar, compare and recommendation sections were removed; inside Houzy, the conversation does that job.

03

Photos in the chat, because hiding them read as hiding something

Phase 2. Seeing photos meant going from carousel to property page to gallery. When buyers typed "show me the bedrooms", Houzy's wording made them think the images were fake or the app was broken. In a market with low trust, a missing photo reads as evasive.

Option AA link out to the property gallery. Quick to build, but it's the same detour that caused the problem.
Option BA fixed carousel or grid. It hides how many photos exist, or leaves broken-looking gaps at 1 or 3.
ChosenPhotos inline, with a layout per count (1 · 2 · 3 · 4 · 4+) and a sentence built from the data: "This property has 2 bedrooms, here are the photos".
  • 1 photo at its own aspect ratio, 2 side by side, 3 as one large and two small, 4+ as a grid with "+N". No count produces an empty cell.
  • "+N" opens the full gallery on that room, with a Close button instead of Back so the chat is preserved underneath.
  • If a room has no photos, Houzy says so for that room only and offers rooms that do. It never gives a blanket "no images".
  • Room synonyms (hall = living room, washroom = bathroom) mean the buyer's words always work.
04

Pills when Houzy can predict, conversation when the buyer needs room

Phase 2. Follow-up questions were buried in prose ("…or specify your maximum budget"). Buyers didn't know what to ask next, and conversations stalled. But turning every question into buttons would make Houzy feel like a form, which is the very thing it replaced.

BeforeChoices written inside the reply. Easy to miss, and the buyer has to type them out.
OptionPills everywhere. Fast, but rigid and form-like, and it quietly takes away free text.
ChosenA rule: show pills only when Houzy can confidently predict the next step (after results, at evaluation and at decision points). Keep conversation for subjective or multi-part intent.
  • Concrete values, not prompts. "2 BHK · 3 BHK · 1 BHK", ordered by popularity, not "Choose BHK".
  • Five inventory rules: no dead-end pills; only values that exist in these results; each dimension in its own group; skip non-choices (if every result is 2 BHK, don't ask); recalculate on every refresh.
  • Pills are shortcuts, never the only path. The text box is always there.
  • Pills are outlined and neutral, so they never compete with the purple Contact button.
Details

Small decisions that add up to trust

An AI fails more often than a form does, so its states and details are part of the main experience.

Never a dead end

  • When nothing matches, each alternative relaxes one filter and shows what it unlocks ("Nearby localities · +9 homes").
  • An option only appears if it unlocks at least 3 homes. When budget is the blocker, Houzy shows the real market price.

Dates, not timestamps

  • No time on AI replies: they arrive in seconds, so a time adds nothing.
  • The date started as a sticky bar; it became a pill that shows only while scrolling, then fades.

Seen and Contacted

  • A small corner tag that never covers price or photo. Contacted beats Seen beats nothing.
  • Seen expires (15 days for resale and rent, 30 for projects). Contacted never does.

Data as cards, not text

  • Price trends lead with the year-on-year verdict and show the change in ₹ per sq ft, which is more honest than a percentage alone.
  • Ratings use sentiment-coloured bars, so strengths and concerns don't look identical.

System thinking25–30 MIN

Bricks foundations, Houzy-owned components

The rule I designed by: Houzy inherits every foundation from Bricks, Housing's design system (type, colour, spacing, radius, the property card's anatomy), but owns the components a conversation needs. It should feel like a native part of Housing, while being clearly the AI part.

Derived from Bricks

Foundations, token for token

  • Type: Rubik via brand_font. Price in title_xl_m (18/20), project name in title_m_m, messages in title_m_r, tags in caption_xs, buttons in label_m_m.
  • Colour: background/brand/primary for Contact and Send only; stroke/brand outline for secondary actions; RERA and Verified tags use text/semantic/success on background/semantic/success.
  • Layout: space/2xs · s · m (4 · 12 · 16), radius/s (8) on buttons, border/default and stroke/primary dividers.
Defined in Houzy

What a conversation needs

  • Asymmetric messages: the buyer's words sit in a white bubble; Houzy's answer sits on the canvas with no bubble, so it reads as content, not chatter.
  • Scoped question: a "↳ Evolvus Phase 1" quote above a question asked from a property page, so you know which home it's about.
  • Floating pill header: back button plus "Houzy · BETA · ⓘ", with no app bar. Content scrolls underneath and the beta status stays visible.
  • Composer states: Default, Fill, Long message; Send turns brand purple only when there's something to send; Stop and Next.
Defined in Houzy

Rich answers

  • "Did you mean one of these?" Each option labelled with its type (Locality, Developer, City, Landmark), paged "1 of 2", with "Something else" or Skip. The conversation map, made visible.
  • AI answer cards: loading skeleton, brochure, locality (Rating ★4/5 and Growth ▲26.7% YoY tiles) and property.
  • Carousel end: a "View more" card that grows as you pull, from a minimum to a maximum state.
  • Quiet tags: Seen and Contacted in grey caption_xs_r, so they never compete with the green RERA tag.
Governance · where it drifted

What the audit found, and what I'd fix

  • The locality picker and options list still use the older Housing tokens (text_colors/grey/500, background_&_surface_colors/low_impact, a hard-coded subtitles/12-400), not Bricks.
  • One chat bubble spec uses Google Sans Flex, a third type system, instead of Rubik.
  • A local Neutral #323232 variable in the header and screens has no Bricks equivalent.
  • Fix: rebind these to Bricks tokens, and propose the chat patterns (composer, answer cards, quiet tags) back to Bricks as a conversational layer, so the next AI surface starts from them.

If I went back

What I'd do differently

  • e.g. design the error states for the property page answer window up front. Engineering had to ask for them.
  • e.g. design V1 around the ML's real limits sooner. Floor plans, photos and streaming slipped out of V1 because the model couldn't support them yet; phase 2 had to add them.
Why this transfers

Designing trust into high-stakes flows

  • Show, don't claim: photos in chat because hiding them read as evasive.
  • Disclosure up front: the AI disclaimer, and RERA before the price.
  • Honest gaps: real market prices instead of a vague "no results".
  • One voice per screen, so users always know who's answering.
