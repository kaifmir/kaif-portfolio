Home buying: themes, gaps and opportunities in the journey

On Housing.com, home buying is still discovery-led and listing-centric, with little help to compare, evaluate or decide. This research moved us from assumptions to evidence about what the platform should intentionally solve.

Why this research

Buyers can find homes on Housing.com. They get little help deciding.

We wanted to understand how buyers actually navigate the journey, where Housing adds value today, and which problems the platform should solve on purpose.

The objective

Separate problems that are perception-based from those that are structurally real, needs a platform solves best from those better handled offline, and areas where Housing has a defensible right to win.

01 Seven themes

Seven things the research made clear

#1

Trust is mis-timed, not missing

Buyers don't distrust Housing. They leave to assemble trust elsewhere, because the platform offers nothing at the critical evaluation moment.

Where it breaks
  1. Buyers can't verify the builder's intent.
  2. They can't validate their own decision, socially or financially.
Defining factors
  • Builder credibilityDelivery history, past project ratings, litigation record
  • Legal & RERA statusLive registration, approvals, litigation flags
  • Pricing fairnessFair-value benchmark vs comparable transacted prices
  • Resident validationVerified post-possession reviews from real buyers
  • Bank approval signalWhich banks have sanctioned loans for this project
What participants said
#2

Curated shortlisting beats discovery breadth

The platform surfaces 100+ listings and buyers collapse. A broker's core value is narrowing that to 2–4 intent-matched options, and Housing has the behavioural data to do this at scale.

Where it breaks
  1. Discovery volume without curation creates paralysis, not confidence.
  2. Buyers can't structure their own comparison: everything is tracked mentally, nothing is saved.
Defining factors
  • Intent signal captureSearch terms, revisit behaviour, time spent per listing
  • Budget & location fitStated vs inferred affordability, plus commute constraints
  • Family preference matchConfiguration, floor and amenity preferences across sessions
  • Comparison trackingSide-by-side saved listings with explicit pros and cons
  • Saved journey memorySession continuity, so no progress is lost on return visits
What participants said
#3

Engage renters before they are buyers

The Renter → FTB → IB evolution is the most structurally important insight in this research. Brokers exploit it from the rental stage, and Housing's rental inventory is an entry point other platforms don't have.

Where it breaks
  1. Buyers enter the journey before they know they're buyers, triggered by life events like marriage, kids or income growth.
  2. Brokers capture this transition early; platforms are absent until the buyer declares intent.
Defining factors
  • Rent-vs-buy decision toolEMI vs rent comparison with area-specific inputs
  • Area appreciation signals‘This area is growing; now is a good time to enter’
  • Readiness nudgesTriggered when renter behaviour suggests buying intent
  • Early relationship CRMLong-term engagement before purchase intent crystallises
  • Life-stage triggersMarriage, a child, a job change: signals that move renters
What participants said
#4

Listings fail at conveying lived experience

Buyers make emotional decisions about spaces they can't yet inhabit, but listings are spec sheets. What buyers need takes developer cooperation or serious content investment to deliver authentically.

Where it breaks
  1. Listings cover specs, not experience: carpet area, not sunlight, noise or commute feel.
  2. The gap is only filled by a site visit, so the platform loses the buyer before validation.
Defining factors
  • Sunlight & orientationUnit-level east/west facing, floor, shadow patterns
  • Noise environmentTraffic, commercial and airport proximity indicators
  • Commute experiencePeak-hour travel time, not just map distance
  • Neighbourhood characterMarket, park and school density: walkability reality
  • Under-construction visualiserA realistic render of the final view, not brochure CGI
What participants said
#5

Investor buyers are unreached, broker-only right now

Investor buyers never use open platforms. They access deals through trusted broker networks, NRI databases and past-client relationships, which needs a fundamentally different product mode.

Where it breaks
  1. Open platforms aren't part of the IB journey: discovery, shortlisting and closure all happen through broker networks.
  2. Their criteria are different: ROI, appreciation and resale, not livability.
Defining factors
  • ROI & yield calculatorGross yield, appreciation potential, rental cover of EMI
  • Micro-market appreciationLocality-level price trend and infrastructure pipeline
  • Exclusive inventory accessPre-launch and broker-curated, not on the open platform
  • Resale potential signalsHistorical price movement for the project or micro-market
  • Network & NRI accessReferral-based onboarding and a trusted community layer
What participants said
#6

Lead quality is broken for the supply side

Brokers and developers both flagged platform leads as low-intent noise. The platform is seen as a top-of-funnel lead generator, not a conversion tool, and that erodes supply-side trust.

Where it breaks
  1. No intent signal travels with the lead, so serious buyers look the same as browsers.
  2. Spam follow-ups from the supply side damage the buyer experience and reinforce distrust.
Defining factors
  • Intent scoring modelA composite of search depth, revisit rate and enquiry timing
  • Hot / warm / cold segmentsTiered lead quality for brokers and developer sales
  • FTB vs IB classificationBuyer type, so leads are routed appropriately
  • Readiness signalsLoan pre-approval started, shortlist saved, site visit requested
  • Conversion likelihoodPredicted time-to-decision from historical behaviour
What participants said
#7

Buyers loop, not funnel: progress resets

First-time buyers cycle through search → shortlist → doubt → restart for months. Each restart erases prior progress, and the platform has no memory to help them pick up where they left off.

Where it breaks
  1. The journey is non-linear and long: buyers revisit projects, change preferences and restart.
  2. With no saved state, every session starts from zero and the buyer drifts to competitors or brokers.
Defining factors
  • Saved shortlist with historyA persistent shortlist across sessions, not wiped on logout
  • ‘You viewed this before’ nudgesReconnect returning buyers to prior shortlists and searches
  • Decision progress trackerShows where the buyer is in their journey
  • Preference drift detectionSurfaces when stated preferences change across sessions
  • Re-engagement triggersPrice drops, new inventory in saved areas, RERA updates
What participants said
02 From insight to product

Turning four themes into product

Trust on the new project page, hotspots for curated discovery, renters primed before they buy, and a memory for returning buyers.

Theme #1 → Trust, designed in

Designing trust into the new project page

Trust is mis-timed, not missing. So we brought the signals buyers were assembling elsewhere onto the page, at the moment of evaluation.

InsightBuyers verify builders and approvals elsewhere
Design moveRERA tags, RERA-linked status and developer ratings on the project page
ResultRolling out on new project pages
  1. 01
    RERA tags, up front

    A RERA badge sits right beside the project name, alongside new launch, zero brokerage and the rating, so legitimacy is the first thing a buyer sees.

  2. 02
    Details that answer the first questions

    Configuration, sizes, average price and status in one scannable block, instead of buried in the listing.

  3. 03
    Status, straight from RERA

    The project status is linked via API to the RERA sites, so "ready to move" is verified, not just claimed.

  4. 04
    A developer profile with a rating

    Who's building it, with a star rating from buyers, answering "will this builder deliver?" on the page itself.

Theme #2 → Hotspot discovery

A curated layer between "everything" and "nothing"

Curated shortlisting beats discovery breadth. So we introduced hotspots: a strata of relevant areas that gives buyers meaningful options while they're still discovering, instead of 100+ listings at once.

Insight100+ listings cause paralysis
Design moveHotspots: a curated layer of relevant areas across home, search, results and map
Result+1% leads per user, and the leads are high-intent

Theme #7 → Picking up where they left off

Remembering the properties buyers come back to

Buyers loop, not funnel, and every restart used to erase their progress. Now a returning buyer sees their history with a property, and how its value has moved since they last saw it.

InsightEvery restart erases progress
Design moveViewing history, price/ROI since the last visit, and Seen / Contacted cards
ResultIn development
  1. 01They returnA buyer comes back to the app after days or weeks away
  2. 02We recognise itThe property, or one just like it, is matched to their viewing history
  3. 03We comparePrice movement and ROI are calculated against their last visit
  4. 04We show the change"Viewed before" with its ROI since then, so the decision moves forward instead of restarting
Card memory

Listings remember what you've done

For buyers who loop, every property card now carries their history, so a returning visit starts from where they left off, not from zero.
